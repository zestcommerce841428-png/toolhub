How this actually protects your text

Your passphrase never leaves your browser and is never stored: it's run through PBKDF2 (250,000 iterations) with a fresh random salt to derive an AES-256-GCM key, which then encrypts your text with a fresh random IV. Both salt and IV travel with the ciphertext in the output — they don't need to be secret, only the passphrase does. Because AES-GCM is authenticated, decrypting with the wrong passphrase or against altered ciphertext fails outright rather than silently producing corrupted-looking output.

There is no "forgot passphrase" recovery — if you lose it, the encrypted text is permanently unrecoverable. That's the same tradeoff every real encryption scheme makes.
